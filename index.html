WHO AM I CHOOSING TO BE?
-PERSONAL TRAITS -MORALS -VALUES -TEMPERAMENT - RESPONSIBILITIES
  1. The foundation to a strong family is the individual parent’s personal way of being. Who are you choosing to be? What are your morals, principles, and values? How do you present yourself to your family and community? Are you responsible and do you make healthy, functional, and safe choices? Sometimes we say, “That’s just how I am,” often because change feels hard or unsafe. Healing-centered growth honors that change is possible. Hope grows when we recognize our power of choice and take small, meaningful steps toward becoming the person—and parent—we want to be.
  2. When challenges show up in one area of the pyramid, it can help to look at the level beneath it. The closer a concern is to the foundation, the more support it may need. Strengthening the base—your own well-being and the co-parent relationship—helps support everything that grows above it.
  3. Understanding key terms matters—especially the difference between discipline and correction. Discipline is about teaching and guiding; correction belongs at the top of the pyramid and should be used less often. When the foundation of connection, modeling, and teaching is strong, children naturally need less correction because they are learning through safety and relationship.
1.6

Hope

Hope Defined: Hope differs from a wish because it involves action. It means believing change is possible and recognizing that our choices—especially small, steady ones—can move us forward. Hope grows when we keep going with support, compassion, and patience.

3 Parts of Hope:

  • The Goal, dream, or desire that we are hoping to obtain or accomplish
  • The Vision of the pathway that we must travel in order to accomplish the goal or dream desired.
  • The Power of Choice must constantly and persistently be used to move the goal along the pathway until that goal or dream is accomplished. The power of choice!

People who are grounded in hope and supported by care learn to look for new pathways when obstacles appear. They recognize that strong emotions, discouragement, past experiences, and disappointment are not weaknesses—but signals that deserve compassion and understanding. With safety, support, and time, we can stay connected to our goals, even when the path forward feels unclear.

Life can include unmet dreams, desires, and goals—not because people are lazy or unwilling, but often because barriers are real, resources are limited, or no one showed us alternative paths. Healing-centered growth honors the power of choice while also recognizing that persistence is a skill that can be learned and strengthened.

Simply put: when people are given support, tools, and belief in their worth, they are far more able to keep going.
